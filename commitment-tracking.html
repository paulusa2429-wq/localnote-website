Commitment tracking

Every promise from every meeting, tracked

Meetings run on promises: "I'll send the revised quote Friday." "Onboarding is included." "We'll confirm the date next week." Then the week moves on and nobody can prove what was agreed. Proofaro keeps a ledger of those commitments — who promised what, by when — drawn from the meeting record itself. It is how you track commitments from meetings without a spreadsheet to maintain or a memory to trust — and how you catch changes in client commitments while they are still cheap to fix.

Promises become a ledger

As meetings are transcribed on your computer, Proofaro collects the commitments in them into one list: the promise, who made it, when it was due, and the exact words behind it. No manual logging, no separate tracker to keep up to date — the ledger is built from what was actually said.

From there it runs itself: later meetings and documents are checked against the open entries, and whatever is due, overdue or changed surfaces in your Morning Queue.

Nothing quietly slips

  • Due dates surface in your day. Your morning queue shows what is due, what is overdue, and what changed since yesterday.
  • Changes get flagged. If a later meeting or document moves a deadline, drops a promise or changes a number, the ledger entry is checked against it.
  • Built for client work. Consultants run whole engagements on promises like these — see how Proofaro for consultants keeps the written scope honest to the call.
  • Answers come with receipts. Ask "what did we agree with the client last month?" and Proofaro answers from your meetings with the original quote attached, so you can check it yourself.

Tracked locally, like everything else

The ledger lives on your computer with the rest of your meeting record. Proofaro works fully offline after a one-time setup download, and no server ever receives your meetings.

What counts as a commitment

A commitment is a promise with an owner and a moment it was made. As each meeting is transcribed on your computer, the promises in it are collected with the person who made them, the date they are due, and the exact words behind them. Nothing has to be typed into a tracker afterwards — if it was said in a meeting Proofaro recorded, it is in the ledger, and if it was not said, the ledger does not invent it.

A worked example: the Acme renewal

On the Acme renewal call, three promises are made. Maya Chen will send the revised quote by Friday. The renewal is $4,000 a year for twelve seats, onboarding included. Dana Park will sign by Friday. Each enters the ledger with its owner, due date and the exact words behind it — nobody typed them in.

On Thursday the quote arrives: $3,200, onboarding billed separately, the signature now due Monday. The open entries are checked against the document, and the changes are flagged with the original quotes attached — the number moved, the included became a charge, the Friday became a Monday. The flags wait in Dana's Morning Queue with a response draft prepared, before a slipped date becomes a slipped renewal.

The promises that hold are tracked with the same honesty: a kept promise closes, and a date still open on the morning it falls due surfaces in the queue instead of slipping silently. Months later, "what did we agree with Acme?" is a lookup with the words attached — not a dig through inboxes.

Your morning queue

The ledger is not a report you remember to open; it arrives in your day. Your Morning Queue shows what is due, what is overdue, and what changed since yesterday, alongside the drafts Proofaro has prepared from yesterday's meetings. The habit takes a minute: scan what is owed, chase what matters, and let the rest of the list hold itself. Promises stop depending on whoever happened to take notes, and "I thought you were sending that" stops being a weekly meeting of its own.

Catching changes in client commitments

Commitments rarely get cancelled out loud — they drift. A deadline moves in a later call, a promise quietly disappears from the follow-up document, a number changes between the meeting and the paperwork. The ledger entry is checked against each later meeting and document, and a change is flagged with the original words as evidence — the same machinery behind Said vs Written, working across your whole history instead of one document. The flag arrives while there is still time to fix things with a sentence — not at the invoice, the renewal, or the argument three months later.

Across every client and project

The ledger does not stop at one meeting, or one engagement. Promises from every recorded meeting sit in the same list, so the commitment a client made in March is still being tracked when the follow-up lands in June — and a promise you made is tracked with the same honesty. Before a meeting, the record is already there: what this client was promised, what they promised you, and what is still open. The follow-through stops living in one person's inbox and memory, and starts living in a record the whole engagement can be checked against. None of it depends on a template, a form, or the discipline of logging calls afterwards — the meetings you already have are the input.

Honest limits

The ledger only knows what was said in meetings Proofaro recorded. A promise made in a corridor, a chat thread or a call on another device is not in it — and the ledger will not invent it. Anything the record cannot confirm is marked unconfirmed rather than tidied into a fact. And Proofaro cannot make anyone keep a promise: it surfaces what is due, flags what changed and prepares the chase-up draft. Sending it — and deciding what a slip means — stays with you.

Questions people ask

How do I track commitments from meetings?

You don't log them: the ledger builds itself from the meeting record. As each meeting is transcribed on your computer, its promises are collected with the person who made them, the date due and the exact words behind them. Due and overdue items surface in your Morning Queue.

How do I catch changes in client commitments?

When a later meeting or document moves a deadline, drops a promise or changes a number, the ledger entry is checked against it and the change is flagged with the original words as evidence — the same machinery as Said vs Written. The flag arrives while a one-sentence reply can still fix it.

What happens when a promised date slips?

The entry does not quietly move with it. The slip is flagged against the original promise — the date that was agreed, in the words it was agreed in — and surfaces in your Morning Queue with a response draft prepared for your review. Nothing is sent without your approval.

Can I ask what was agreed with a client?

Yes — and the answer comes with the original quote attached to each commitment, so you can check it against the words yourself instead of trusting a summary.
