ONE MEETING, START TO FINISH

One meeting, start to finish.

This is the Acme renewal call — the same fictional sample meeting built into LocalNote, with Maya Chen and Dana Park of Acme. Follow it for one week: what was ready before it, what happened in it, and everything LocalNote did with it afterwards. One meeting, the whole product.

Every name, number and line on this page is fictional — it is the app's own sample data, shown so you can see the work, not just the claims. Nothing here is a real customer or a real call.
1 · BEFORE THE MEETING

The brief is ready before anyone sits down.

Fifteen minutes before the call, LocalNote has already read your earlier meetings with Acme. The brief is short on purpose: what is still open, what is already decided, and what to listen for.

Brief · Acme renewal call
Still open — from the kickoff callDana Park was to confirm the final seat count with finance. It never landed, so it is on today's brief, not buried in old notes.
Decision on recordThe renewal agreement from March: onboarding is billed separately, at the standard day rate. That is the record this meeting will be checked against.
Also outstandingLegal's review of the renewal terms — promised, not yet confirmed. And a competitor has quoted $3,200 for the same seats; their migration takes three weeks.

Also at work here: the same brief exists for every person you meet — their promises to you, yours to them, and what changed since last time — and questions you saved can be run again before you walk in.

2 · DURING

Recorded on this computer. No bot in the room.

LocalNote records from the app, with a consent reminder shown and the consent written to an encrypted log. Nobody joins the call as a guest, and the audio never leaves the machine. The transcript is written on-device, with a name to each voice:

Transcript · Acme renewal call (excerpt)
0:18 · Maya Chen“Thanks. Our current plan renews on the 30th, and we want to keep the same seats — twelve in total.”
1:15 · Dana Park“Four thousand is more than we budgeted. A competitor quoted us thirty-two hundred for the same seats.”
2:15 · You“I understand. What if we keep the price at four thousand and include the onboarding and the migration help at no extra cost?”
4:15 · Dana Park“One more thing: legal has to review the renewal terms. I'll confirm their review by Wednesday.”
4:45 · Maya Chen“Decision then: we renew at four thousand, with onboarding included, and we sign by Friday.”
6:45 · You“Great. To recap: four thousand a year, twelve seats, onboarding included, signature by Friday.”

Also at work here: speaker labels fill in on their own, words the transcription is unsure about are flagged instead of hidden, a name or jargon word you teach once is fixed everywhere — even in past meetings — and a dead battery never loses the recording.

3 · THE CHECK

The summary is checked against what was actually said.

After the call, the summary is matched line by line against the transcript. Anything the meeting never backed is stamped, in the open — never quietly dropped, never quietly kept. Two lines in this summary don't survive the check:

Summary · Acme renewal call, checked
UNCONFIRMED“Acme agreed to match the competitor's $3,200 price.”Nobody agreed to that. The competitor's price was mentioned; the renewal was decided at $4,000. The line is stamped, and the exact words behind the real decision stay one tap away.
UNCONFIRMED“Legal has approved the renewal terms.”The meeting said the opposite: legal still has to review them, and Dana will confirm by Wednesday. An approval that hasn't happened is not written down as one that has.
BACKED BY THE TRANSCRIPT“Renewal: $4,000 a year for twelve seats, onboarding and migration help included. Signature by Friday.”Said at 4:45, recapped at 6:45 — in the meeting's own words.

This is the same check you can run on this site. Run it on a summary of your own →

Also at work here: summaries in English, Spanish and Hindi — and wherever a quote is translated, the translation sits under the original words. The original is the record; the translation is only a guide.

5 · A DECISION CHANGED

The room decided something the record didn't say.

Remember the March agreement: onboarding is billed separately. This meeting just gave it away free. Work Guardian puts the two side by side, quoted word for word, and waits — the record is not edited, and nothing is treated as agreed until you have seen both:

Fictional sample
Decision changed — onboardingThe approved record says: “Onboarding is billed separately, at the standard day rate.”

This meeting said: “What if we keep the price at four thousand and include the onboarding and the migration help at no extra cost?” — and Maya confirmed the decision on those terms.Waiting in My Day, under “Needs review”. The quote and the follow-up use the new terms only if you confirm the change.

Also at work here: the same check catches scope that crept, dates that moved and owners that changed — and you can save both sides of any change as a receipt, in their exact words, as one document.

6 · THE FOLLOW-UP, READY

The follow-up is written. It is not sent.

By the time the call ends, the follow-up is already drafted from the meeting itself — the decision, the four promises, the dates. It waits for your eyes. LocalNote has never sent anything on its own, and it doesn't start here:

Draft · Follow-up to Maya Chen and Dana Park
Subject: Acme renewal — confirmed at $4,000, onboarding includedHi Maya, Dana — confirming what we decided today: Acme renews at $4,000 a year for twelve seats, with onboarding and migration help included at no extra cost, and signature by Friday.

Next steps: the revised quote reaches you by Friday (Maya); legal's review is confirmed by Wednesday (Dana); the onboarding checklist reaches you by Thursday (me); kickoff is scheduled for next Monday.

Thanks, both.

A draft, prepared by LocalNote. Nothing is sent until you tap.

Also at work here: before a draft goes anywhere, it is checked and anything doubtful is flagged “Needs a check”; a meeting can become a polished PDF or a working document the same way; and when the written quote later goes out, it can be checked against what was actually said — agreed, contradicted, or never mentioned.

8 · A WEEK LATER

The other side confirms the exact words.

The decision didn't stay inside LocalNote. It went to Maya as one small file — no install, no account, nothing uploaded — and her answer came back bound to those exact words:

Fictional sample
Confirmed — Acme renewal, decision packet version 1“We renew at four thousand dollars a year for twelve seats, with onboarding and migration help included at no extra cost, and we sign by Friday.”Maya Chen confirmed the exact words in the packet. If the packet changes, this answer stays with version 1 — it is never carried over quietly.

Identity not verified — the packet says so itself. A confirmation is a record of an answer, never a legal signature. And the confirmed packet can now propose updating the approved record — the onboarding line from beat 5 — through Work Guardian, for your approval. The loop closes where it started: with you deciding.
